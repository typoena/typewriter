## The power path

A **BQ25896** owns everything between the USB-C charge port and the cell. It
selects the source, limits input current, runs its own buck to SYS, and charges
through an internal BATFET — so there is no discrete FET, no Schottky, and no
separate fuel gauge on the board. SYS feeds a TPS63001 buck-boost whose enable is
tied high in hardware: **3V3 is always live**, and the firmware cannot switch it
off.

```
USB-C charge ──VBUS──► BQ25896 ──BAT──► [JST-PH] LiPo 3700 mAh
                          │ I2C 0x6B (SDA 17 / SCL 18)
                          └──SYS──┬──► TPS63001 ──► 3V3  (MCU, EPD, uSD, CH343P)
                                  └──► TPS61023 ──► 5V   (keyboard, EN ← IO41)
3V3 ──► AO3401A ──► 3V3_SD ──► uSD   (EN ← IO40)
```

**The switch is latching — two states, not a press.** Closed pulls `PWR_SENSE`
(IO21) low and means *on*; open lets the internal pull-up take it high and means
*off*. The firmware follows that level; there is no press duration to measure
anywhere.

**Off is the ESP32's deep sleep.** The switch cuts nothing itself. Flipping it on
drives IO21 low, which is the `ext0` wake-up — and that is why the pin has to stay
inside GPIO 0–21: only RTC GPIOs wake an S3 from deep sleep. Flipping it off
leaves the pin high, which is exactly what lets the shutdown arm `ext0` for low
without waking the chip straight back up.

The switch also carries an LED (`BTN_LED`, IO38, active-high through a 2N7002).
Lit means awake; it goes out the instant the switch reads off, a second before
the panel can say so.

**The firmware will not sleep on a switch it has never seen say *on*.** An
unplugged `J2` reads exactly like a switch in the off position, and a machine that
honoured it would sleep on every boot with nothing able to wake it. So the first
*on* reading arms the switch, and only an armed switch can shut the machine down.
A board with no switch fitted stays on — recoverable — instead of unreachable.

> **Screen truth table** — the off card ("typoena / off - flip the switch to
> wake") means off. Anything else means on. E-paper is bistable, so the panel is
> the power display at zero cost, and it keeps being one with the rails down.

## Behaviour contract, by state

1. **Off, on mains** → the machine sleeps, the cell charges. The charger runs
   without firmware: `/CE` is strapped low, so a board charges at its power-on
   defaults even on a first power-up that never reaches an app.
2. **On, on mains** → the machine runs off the input and the cell charges
   alongside. The BQ25896's power path does this itself; nothing to detect.
3. **On, mains pulled** → the internal BATFET carries the load with no
   interruption. Nothing in firmware sees it beyond the next telemetry read.
4. **Flip on while off** → IO21 goes low, `ext0` wakes the chip, `main` raises
   the switched rails, and boot proceeds normally. The switch stays closed for
   the whole session, which is what arms it.
5. **Flip off while on** → the LED goes out at once, every dirty buffer is saved,
   the off card is painted, the card is unmounted, the panel goes to its own deep
   sleep (`0x10`/`0x03`), the switched rails drop, and the chip sleeps. An
   **unnamed** dirty scratch is parachuted to `/sd/local/unsaved.md` rather than
   blocking: the switch already reads off, so refusing would strand the machine
   on with no second flip left to stop it.
6. **Flip back on mid-shutdown** → the sequence finishes anyway. Arming `ext0`
   for low on a pin that reads low would boot-loop, so the shutdown waits the
   level out (up to 10 s) and the machine comes back as a normal wake.
7. **Cell under 3.3 V, three reads running** → the machine runs (5) on its own.
   A save and an off card beat a brownout mid-write. The switch still reads *on*
   afterwards, so flipping it off and on again is what restarts the machine once
   there is charge.

## Hazards

- ⚠️ **Ship mode is unreachable, deliberately.** `REG09[5]` (`BATFET_DIS`) would
  give a true zero-draw off state, but the only ways back out of it are `/QON` or
  a fresh adapter — and `/QON` is `SW1`, a board-mounted button inside the case.
  Setting it would leave a machine the writer cannot switch on.
- ⚠️ **The charger's I2C watchdog must stay off.** Its default is 40 s and it
  reloads every writable register with its power-on default when it expires —
  which it would, every time, since the MCU spends hours asleep and nothing kicks
  it. `Bq25896::configure` disables it before anything else.
- ⚠️ **A hung firmware cannot be switched off by the user.** The switch is a
  signal, not a contactor, and nothing else cuts power. Recovery is `SW1`
  (`/QON`) inside the case, or a flat cell. Wiring the user's switch to `/QON` as
  well — a diode-OR onto the same net — is the hardware fix if this ever bites.
- The µSD and keyboard rails are **off at reset**. Anything that mounts the card
  or installs the USB host stack before `Rails::bring_up` finds dead hardware.
