# Enclosure — Typoena body

A 3D-printable case for [**Typoena**](../../README.md), the distraction-free DIY
writing machine. The e-paper strip sits on a reclined **deck**, where a
typewriter's paper would be; you bring your own keyboard for the front. There is
no platen part (it complicates the print); the rounded back-top edge nods to one.

> **Status: outer form and screen clamp proven on a print; the cavity
> follows the fabricated mainboard and has not been printed against it.** The
> wedge, the deck and the glass retention are worked out against the real
> hardware and every number there is measured — the **3700 mAh** cell included.
> The board block — mounting, ports, button — is cut straight from
> [`typoena-devboard.kicad_pcb`](../pcb/devboard/typoena-devboard.kicad_pcb), so
> its dimensions are exact and its *fits* are untried: **print the I/O coupon
> first**. The machine's over-extrusion is corrected **in the slicer** and not in
> the model, so **[MANUFACTURING.md](MANUFACTURING.md)** is required reading
> before any print. The 12 heat-set inserts are untried — see **Open questions**.

## Render

Needs [OpenSCAD](https://openscad.org). From `hardware/`:

```sh
just render   # regenerate every case/renders/*.png
just stl      # export every STL: body / bracket / baseplate / coupon / assembly
just stl-io   # re-export just the back-wall I/O fit coupon (quick test print)
just stl-feet # export the stick-on feet (deferred — see feet_mode)
just open     # open the model in OpenSCAD
```

The meshes aren't tracked — they're 5 MB of ASCII that git rewrites line by line
on any geometry change. For a shareable set, tag instead: `git tag case-vX.Y.Z &&
git push origin case-vX.Y.Z` has CI export from the tagged commit and publish
`stl.zip`, `stl-kb.zip` and the I/O coupon to
[Releases](https://github.com/typoena/typewriter/releases).

`show` accepts `assembled` · `body` · `bracket` · `baseplate` · `feet` ·
`print_plate` · `section` (vertical cut) · `plan` (exploded horizontal) ·
`plan_up` / `plan_down` (each half alone) · `io_coupon` (the I/O fit test print).

## Dimensions

Baked into the model from the datasheets:

- **Panel (GDEY0579T93):** glass 150.92 × 56.94 × 1.0 mm, active area
  139.00 × 47.74 mm, pitch 0.1755 mm.
- **Mainboard** (99 W × 45 D × 1.6 mm, corners R3): the whole machine on one
  PCBA — BQ25896 power path, the panel's power stage, µSD, both USB-C. **Ø3.7
  hole 5 mm in from each corner**, which is a #6-32 clearance. It lies **along
  the back wall**, ports out.

  **Read its X off the canvas and you get it backwards.** KiCad's Y points down,
  so the port edge is drawn at the *bottom*; laying the board in with its ports at
  the **back** turns it 180° in plan. Every board-local X in the model is
  therefore `u = 116 − kicad_x`, from the board's **left edge as installed** — so
  `J4` (KiCad x 110) ends up 2…7 mm from the **left** edge, and the devkit
  (KiCad x 15…78) fills the **right** half.
- **ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1**, plugged into the mainboard's two 1×22 rows over its
  right half (`u` 38…101): **13.5 mm of stack** over the board face (socket 8.5 +
  board 1.6 + can 3.4) and the tallest thing in the cavity. Its antenna end
  overhangs the board's **right** edge by 2.3 mm, which is the widest the
  assembly ever gets.
- **Battery:** LiPo 3700 mAh, 94 × 32 × 10.3 mm, flat in the front-right.
- **Body:** 176 W × 104 D, 28 mm front → 62 mm back, deck reclined ~21°. Walls
  2.4 mm, deck 2.6 mm, corner radius 8 mm.

## How the hardware goes in (glueless)

No glue on the fragile 1 mm glass; every part stays serviceable.

![Bare body shell — screen recess through the deck, FPC slot on the left edge](renders/body.png)

**Screen.** The glass drops into the deck recess from behind (the walls locate it
in X/Y). Front to back the stack is: deck **bezel lip** (covers the inactive
border only, `lip_t = 2.4 mm`) → **glass** → non-adhesive closed-cell **foam**
gasket (`foam_t = 5 mm` free, squashed to `foam_c = 3.5` — a 30% squash) → printed
**bracket**
screwed to 4 bosses. The lip stops the glass falling out the front; the bracket
stops it dropping into the cavity. The FPC folds back through an internal
clearance on the **left** (kept under the bezel, so invisible outside) and down
into the cavity on its way to the board. For a screwless build, cantilever clips work but point-load the glass
edge; I'd default to foam + bracket.

The bosses' **free ends are the bracket's seating face** (`br_seat = 6.9 mm` below
the deck's outer face). The screw pulls the bracket onto that shoulder and stops,
so the foam squash — and therefore the clamp on a brittle panel — is set by
geometry rather than by how hard you turn the screwdriver. That is also why the
foam is 5 mm and not 1: the seat has to sit far enough behind the glass to leave
the boss room for a **heat-set insert** (see *Fasteners* below). The bore stops
`pilot_skin = 2.1 mm` short of the deck's outer face, so nothing shows on the side
the user looks at — and that skin, not the thread, is what set `lip_t`.

Locating tolerances: the pocket gives the glass **0.25 mm of play per side** in X
and Y, against a window margin of 1.00 mm (X) / 0.50 mm (Y) over the active area —
so a drifted panel shifts the bezel by a quarter of a millimetre and can never clip
a pixel. Don't try to make the bracket locate anything either way: the pocket
locates, the bracket only clamps. Its clearance holes are **Ø3.9 on a Ø3.5 shank** —
drive the screws through the bracket rather than expecting it to drop over them.

![Midline section: glass clamped between front lip and rear foam + bracket](renders/section.png)
![The bracket — four corner holes, window clears the active area, left edge relieved for the FPC](renders/bracket.png)

**Fasteners.** Every screwed joint in the machine uses one family: a
**ruthex RX-6-32×3.8** brass heat-set insert (Ø4.7 body, 3.8 long, datasheet bore
**Ø4.8 × 4.8 min**, **1.8 mm minimum wall**) taking a **#6-32** pan screw — 7 mm
of thread under a flat 1 mm × Ø6.5 head. Both parts are measured, not
catalogue numbers. All three joints get *opened*: the baseplate on every service,
the bracket on every glass swap, the board whenever it comes out — and a
self-tapped thread in PLA is good for a handful of cycles before it's a stripped
hole in a 10-hour print.

| | bracket → deck (×4) | baseplate → body (×4) | board → standoff (×4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| insert lives in | the body, under the deck | the body, from below | the **baseplate**, from above |
| boss | Ø8.9, **2.05 mm** of wall | rectangular pad, **2.6 mm** to the free faces | Ø8.9 standoff, **2.05 mm** of wall |
| bore | Ø4.8 × 4.8 blind, **2.1 mm of deck** left over it | Ø4.8 × 6.6 blind, 3.4 mm of roof | Ø4.8 × 6.4 blind, bottoming on the plate — **3.2 mm** under it |
| screw crosses | 3.6 mm of bracket | 1.4 mm of plate | 1.6 mm of PCB |
| thread in brass | **3.4 mm** | **3.8 mm** (the whole insert) | **3.8 mm** (the whole insert) |
| head | **no lamage** — bears on the bracket's face, inside the case | in a **lamage** in the plate's underside, flush with the desk — **drilled, not printed** (see [Drilling the baseplate](#drilling-the-baseplate)) | **no lamage** — bears on the PCB, Ø6.5 on a Ø3.7 hole |

Every bore is a **plain Ø4.8 cylinder** — no relief, no step, no lead-in for the
insert's head end: the iron melts its own seat, so the bore is not a clearance fit
and nothing about the insert's outline is modelled. There is no counterbore left
anywhere in the model: the baseplate's four screw passages come out of the printer
**solid** and are drilled afterwards.

That Ø4.8 is what Ruthex asks for around a Ø4.7 body: the insert melts its own
seat, so this is a grip, not a clearance fit. No insert has been set yet — judge
the first one on a part sliced with the XY compensation, since an uncompensated
bore lands ~0.16 mm under and the brass would be pressed into a hole that tight.

Every bore is deliberately **deeper than the insert** (they have to swallow the
screw tip), and with the relief gone **nothing in the part stops the press**. Sink
each insert flush with the boss's free end by eye or with a depth stop on the iron:
that face is a seating face on all three joints — proud brass spends the foam
preload on the bracket, rocks the plate on the baseplate, and tilts the board on
its standoffs.

**Twelve inserts, one screw.** The board is in the family too: its four holes are
a #6-32 clearance, so it screws *down* into an insert in each standoff and the
same **#6-32 × 7** drives every joint in the machine. That is what took the
drill off the baseplate's standoffs — the bore they need is Ø4.8, which a printer
holds, where the Ø1.6 pilot of a self-tapper is two or three perimeters wide and
comes out whatever size the extrusion width allows.

The depth budget is why `lip_t` went 1.4 → 2.4 and `bracket_t` 2.6 → 3.6. Between
the bracket's seat and the deck's *outer face* there is only `br_seat`, and the
bore eats it from below: 6.9 = bore 4.8 + skin 2.1. At the old numbers the bore came
to 5.4 against a `br_seat` of 5.9 — **0.5 mm** of skin on the machine's showpiece
face, with the screw tip arriving under it and the iron melting brass into it.
There is nowhere else to buy that skin, so it came half from the visible lip (a
deeper bezel well) and half from the invisible bracket, and the
glass ends up 1 mm deeper in a stiffer clamp. `typoena-case.scad` **asserts** the
walls, the skin, the roof and the thread engagement — a plausible-looking edit to
any of those four numbers fails the render instead of the print.

**Board.** Mount everything to the **baseplate** on the bench, then close from
below.

The mainboard sits on **four Ø8.9 printed standoffs**, `standoff_h` = **7 mm**
tall, one under each corner hole, and is pulled down onto them by four #6-32 into
their heat-set inserts. The standoff height is set by the insert rather than by
the gap: the bore is 6.4 mm deep from the standoff's free end and bottoms on the
plate, which leaves 3.2 mm of material under it.

That 7 mm gap is the **wiring bay**. The panel's flex crosses it west to east,
and the battery, button and lamp pigtails share it.

- The board lands **back-left**: `x` 18…117, `y` 55.8…100.8, i.e. hard against
  the back wall and as far left as the back-left baseplate boss allows.

  **Y is set by the ports.** The two USB-C shells stand **1.3 mm past the board's
  back edge** and have to end up *inside* the wall opening rather than behind the
  wall, so the board is pushed back until only **0.8 mm** is left between its edge
  and the wall's inner face. That puts the receptacle mouths 0.5 mm into the
  wall's thickness, 0.3 mm behind the floor of the cable-boot pocket — as close
  to full plug engagement as a 2.4 mm wall gets.

  **X is set by the flex.** The panel's ribbon leaves the glass on the **left**
  and drops through the deck slot at `x` 3.8…13.8; `J4`, the 24-pin FFC that takes
  it, is at the board's **left** end — body at `x` 20.1…24.5, ribbon entering its
  mouth at 25.3 heading **west**. The two want to be as close as they can get, and
  what stops the board at `x` = 18 is the back-left baseplate boss, whose free
  face is at 16; the model asserts the 2 mm between them.

  The run: down through the slot into the **front-left plenum**, east across the
  plenum floor, up over the board's front edge (its left quadrant is clear — the
  JST row starts at `u` 36) and back west into `J4` at (25, 75). Because the
  connector opens east, the flex has to overshoot and fold back on itself — a
  plain 180° reverse bend in the vertical plane, no mitres. Call it **~90 mm**
  of flex with the bends, against a panel ribbon of 20.

  The **DevKitC-1** lies over the board's right half, 8.5 mm up on its headers,
  with its antenna end overhanging the board edge. Nothing is put beside that end,
  and its own USB-C — the machine's programming port — points into the cavity:
  taking the baseplate off brings the whole board assembly out with it, which is
  how you reach it.

  Height is slack here, because the stack stands at the tall end of the wedge: it
  tops out at **24.7 mm** off the plate's underside against a ceiling of **43.9**
  over the board's front edge, so it clears by 19 mm. The model asserts 2.5.
- **Ports**, from the left: µSD at `x` 44.2, keyboard USB-C at 72.0, charge
  USB-C at 89.9 — the board's own spacing, carried over from the PCB file, so the
  cluster follows the board and nothing else can move it. Centres sit at
  `z` 11.91 (µSD card) and 12.83 (USB-C).

  The **µSD needs its pocket to be usable.** The socket is a push-pull Molex
  1040310811 whose mouth sits 1.92 mm *behind* the board's edge, and a card
  protrudes only 4 mm from that mouth — less than the wall is thick. However the
  board is placed, the card stops short of the outer face. So the µSD gets a
  **26 × 11 finger pocket** in the outer face, 1.6 mm deep like the USB-C boots,
  and an **18 × 3.5 opening** through its floor: the card ends level with the
  pocket floor (0.5 mm proud), and the 3.5 mm of open slot either side of an
  11 mm card is what a fingernail hooks. That clearance *is* the grip — it is not
  slack, and it is the first thing to judge on the coupon.
- **Power on/off switch** — a latching push button (push-on / push-off) through
  the back wall (`pwr_*`), Ø13.5 barrel, centre at `x` **132.3** (43.7 mm in from
  the right outer face), on the USB-C centreline. It carries **gate signal only**
  (µA) — it drives the soft-power latch that opens the load path, so the machine
  is genuinely off between sessions instead of draining the LiPo, and firmware
  still shuts down cleanly on flip-off; contract in
  [v0.10](../../docs/plan/v0.10-battery-and-sleep.md#power-switch--power-path--decided-2026-07-26).
  It sits clear of the typing path. It is a **loose part wired back to `J2`** on
  the board, with its lamp on `J3`.

  Nothing stands behind it, so its three rules are all clearances and all
  asserted: the Ø14 nut — the widest thing behind the panel, not the barrel —
  keeps 6 mm to the **devkit's** overhanging antenna end (`x` 119.3, which is what
  it has to clear, not the PCB edge at 117), 20.7 mm to the back-right screw boss,
  and 3.2 mm over the baseplate. The back face is planar only to `x` = 168
  (`W - corner_r`), which is the other end of its travel.

  No reset/BOOT button is exposed (S3 auto-download makes both recovery-only);
  like the devkit's own USB-C they're reached by opening the case.

![Back elevation — µSD, keyboard, charge and the button, all on one line](renders/back.png)
- **LiPo** flat across the **front** in a baseplate cage (foam/VHB does the
  rest) — the shallow (28 mm) front of the wedge is dead space the board stack
  can't use, and **3700 mAh is the biggest flat cell that fits it**
  (94 × 32 × 10.3); the heaviest part up front also keeps the centre of gravity
  low and forward. Its leads reach `J1` on the board's own front edge, ~25 mm
  away. The latching power switch keeps that capacity from bleeding between
  sessions.

  The cage is **not symmetric**. A **wall** stands at the cell's left end and two
  **nibs** sit at its mid-length: the cell goes in from the right, passes between
  the nibs — which hold it in Y only — and slides left until it stops on the wall.
  The nibs are at mid-length because that is where a pouch cell bows, and it is
  the one spot from which neither end of the cell can lever itself out.

  X is boxed both ways. On the left is the **flex plenum** — the front-left floor
  the panel's ribbon drops into and coils its slack in — and on the right the
  front-right screw boss, which is a rigid printed corner at the cell's own height
  and must never meet a pouch cell:

  | | x |
  | --- | --- |
  | flex plenum, right edge (`plenum_x1`) | 56 |
  | cage wall (`bat_wall_t` = 2) | 56 … 58 |
  | cell (94 long) | **58 … 152** |
  | nibs (`bat_nib_x`) | 105 |
  | front-right screw boss, free face | 160 |

  That is **8 mm** of air ahead of the cell, asserted (`battery: the cell runs
  into the front-right screw boss`) because `bat_x0` is set 300 lines from
  `post_xy` and a plausible edit to either would close it in silence. In Y the
  cell runs 6.4…38.4, and the **17.4 mm corridor** between it and the board's
  front edge is the flex's east-west run — also asserted.
- The baseplate screws up into **four bosses**, one at each corner, all on the
  same 3 mm inset off the corner tangent. Each is a rectangular pad **fused into
  the two walls it sits against** — the box in `post_box` is driven straight
  through the shell and `body_outer()` trims it flush, so the fusion can't drift
  when `wall` or `corner_r` moves. They start at `bp_t` so the plate seats *under*
  them; the 2.6 mm void that leaves is the plate's own volume, and the boss's
  first layer bridges off the wall. They are only `post_h` = **10 mm** tall: the
  walls carry the boss, so height is purely about clearing the Ø4.8 × 6.6 insert
  bore with a roof left on it. Running them to the deck was 67% more plastic
  propping nothing the walls weren't already carrying. The insert goes in from
  **below**, into the boss face the plate seats against — the iron reaches it
  straight down through the open bottom of the shell, so press all four *before*
  any board goes in.

  Each screw keeps **4.9 mm of plate rim** outboard of its Ø6.9 lamage for the
  head to pull against; that rim also absorbs the wander of a lamage that is
  drilled by hand. **No boss may stand under the board** — they run 3 mm past the
  board's underside — which is what the back pair costs: it is the back-left one
  that fixes how far left the board can go.

**Assembly order.**

0. **Drill the baseplate** — 4 screw passages, the only feature it does not
   print. Mark their positions through the still-empty Ø4.8 baseplate boss bores
   first; doing that before the inserts go in is what makes the marking possible.
   See [Drilling the baseplate](#drilling-the-baseplate).
1. **Press the 12 inserts**: on the bare body, 4 into the bracket bosses under the
   deck and 4 into the baseplate bosses (from below); on the baseplate, 4 into the
   standoffs (from above). Iron at ~250 °C, straight in, stop when the brass is
   **flush with the boss's face** — every bore is deeper than the insert, so
   nothing bottoms out for you. The bracket four point at the deck's visible face
   with 2.1 mm of PLA behind them — these are the ones to go slowly on.
2. Lay glass into the deck recess, add foam, screw the bracket to the 4 bosses
   (#6-32 × 7). Drive them until the bracket sits flat on the boss shoulders and
   stop — the shoulder is the stop, not the foam.
3. Screw the mainboard down onto its four standoffs (#6-32 × 7, ports facing the
   back); set the LiPo into its cage — slide it in from the right, past the two
   nibs, until it stops on the wall, and plug it into `J1`.
4. Connect the flex: through the deck slot, east along the wiring bay, up over the
   board's front edge and into `J4`, with the slack coiled in the front-left
   plenum. Run the button and lamp pigtails to `J2` / `J3`.
5. Screw the baseplate up into the four bosses.

### Drilling the baseplate

**The four body screws are the only thing the plate does not print.** Everything
else it carries — the standoffs, their Ø4.8 insert bores, the battery cage — comes
off the bed finished.

The lamage is a flat-bottomed pocket in the plate's *first* layers, so the print has
to bridge from Ø7.4 back in to Ø3.9 over open air, and the seat the head pulls
against ends up being whatever that bridge sagged to. A drill gives a flat seat.

Two features per hole, from the plate's **underside** (the face that meets the
desk):

| | Ø | depth | what it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| through hole | **3.9** (Ø4 bit) | through 2.6 | #6-32 shank clearance — the screw is turned through it |
| lamage | **6.9** (Ø7 bit) | **1.2**, flat-bottomed | the Ø6.5 × 1 pan head, finishing 0.2 **below** the face |

Flat-bottomed, not a countersink: the head is a flat 1 mm pan and a cone would only
touch it on one rim circle. The head must finish below the underside — with
`feet_mode = "none"` that face is what the machine stands on, and a proud head makes
it rock on three of four points. Drill the lamage **first**, then the through hole
through its centre: a twist bit's point needs full material to centre on.

Positions, from the plate's front-left corner (the long edge is the front; the plate
is 170.7 × 98.7):

| screw | x | y |
| --- | --- | --- |
| front-left | 8.35 | 8.35 |
| front-right | 162.35 | 8.35 |
| back-left | 8.35 | 90.35 |
| back-right | 162.35 | 90.35 |

Better than measuring: **transfer from the body, before the inserts go in.** The
four boss bores are plain Ø4.8 holes at that point, open from below and pointing
straight down at the seated plate — sit the plate in the shell and mark their
centres through them (a Ø4.8 transfer punch, or a pointed marker and then a centre
punch). Press the inserts afterwards; once the brass is in, its ~Ø2.9 thread no
longer lines a bit up on anything. Every screw keeps 4.9 mm of plate rim outboard
of its lamage, which is the wander budget for doing this by hand.

The keyboard variant's bay posts (`bay_posts()` in `typoena-case-kb.scad`) are the
one place a drilled pilot survives: its keyboard PCB takes M2, and **Ø1.6 is two or
three perimeters wide**, so the printer rounds the hole to whatever its extrusion
width allows and the self-tapper arrives at a hole of unknown size. A bit cuts 1.6.

## Tune first

- **`Hb` (back height) → deck angle.** 18–22° is typewriter-shallow; raise toward
  ~28–35° if the screen reads too edge-on when sitting close.
- **Everything else is pinned to a part.** The cell (`bat_*`) and the panel's
  active-area offset (`active_off_x/y`) are measured; the board and port numbers
  (`pcb_*`, `port_*`, `usbc_*`, `sd_*`) are read off the PCB file. Move them only
  against a new part or a new board revision.

## Print notes

![The three printed parts — body, bracket, baseplate](renders/print.png)

- Material PLA/PETG. Body in matte **sage** (`#B6CEB4`), bracket/base in cream or
  brass — the two-tone reads "typewriter" for a filament swap.
- 2.4 mm walls + open bottom keep material low.
- **Print the I/O coupon first.** `just stl-io` exports `io-coupon.stl` — a flat
  slice of the real back wall carrying only the four openings (2× USB-C with their
  cable-boot pockets, the µSD slot in its finger pocket, the power button) at their
  real spacing and wall thickness. Print it face-down (no support, ~45 min), then
  dry-fit it **against the assembled board**: the USB-C shells stand inside their
  openings now, so the coupon checks alignment and not just size. Adjust
  `usbc_*` / `sd_*` / `port_fit` / `pwr_fit` before committing to the body print.
  At 45 minutes a round it is not a guess-and-reprint loop: caliper the parts, then
  size the openings loose so one print clears.

![The I/O fit coupon — charge, keyboard, µSD, power button](renders/io-coupon.png)
- Print body deck-up (or on its back) for little/no support; bracket and baseplate
  print flat.
- **The STLs are nominal — the slicer carries the correction.** The XY
  compensation is not optional and not in the model; a part sliced without it
  will not assemble. Value: **[MANUFACTURING.md](MANUFACTURING.md)**.
- **Check the fit that goes tight first:** the baseplate must drop into the shell
  with ~0.25 mm of play a side, no force. Both its mating faces are printed, so it
  loses twice whatever the process loses. Worth repeating on every new filament.
- **The deck ships blank** — `name_on = false`. The engraved `TYPOENA` nameplate
  is still modelled and comes back with that one flag; it sits in the band between
  the front edge and the screen, the only free deck area there is. If it does come
  back it needs `name_font` installed (**Monaspace Krypton**, `brew install --cask
  font-monaspace`, then `fc-cache -f` so the CLI finds it) and something to make
  the recess read: a swipe of paint pen, or a 3–4 layer filament swap across the
  band mid-print.
- **No feet on this version** (`feet_mode = "none"`). They are modelled and can
  come back as their own part with `feet_mode = "separate"` + `just stl-feet` —
  four Ø14 × 3.5 discs, the front pair concentric with the screw bosses and bored
  at the lamage Ø so a **driver** still reaches the recessed head (a shank-sized
  hole would put it out of reach and the plate could never come off). Glue them on
  *after* drilling, and off the drilled holes rather than off the model. What they must *not* go back to
  is `"fused"`: hanging off the plate they leave it resting on four small discs,
  turning the whole baseplate into a 3.5 mm overhang that wants support across its
  footprint, standoffs and nibs floating above it. As it stands all three parts
  print flat-face-down with every feature growing upward off the bed — no support
  anywhere.

## Open questions

Everything settled is in the sections above; the model `assert`s the numbers a
plausible edit could break, so those fail the render rather than the print.

- [ ] **Does the 100 mm extension still reach?** The panel's own ribbon is 20 mm
      and the glass back plane sits ~38 mm off the floor at mid-deck, so the drop
      alone is ~35. `J4` is close — its mouth is at (25, 75), a few centimetres
      from where the flex lands — but it **opens east**, so the flex overshoots
      and folds back on itself, and the round trip puts the run near **90 mm with
      the bends**. That is inside 100 with nothing to spare; a 150 mm part would
      coil the difference in the plenum. Measure it against the real adapter
      before ordering, and check the adapter itself sits in the plenum
      (`plenum_x1` = 56, with the 17.4 mm corridor in front of the board behind
      it).
- [ ] **Extension contrast risk, and it grows with the length.** On these
      COG-on-flex panels the charge-pump capacitors live on the driver board and
      the FPC carries the *generated* high-voltage rails (VGH/VGL/VSH/VSL) back to
      the COG. Added length adds resistance on exactly those rails, and the
      classic symptom is faint or ghosty output rather than an outright failure —
      so it can pass a smoke test and still look wrong. Run a full black/white
      refresh through the extension on the bench and compare against the direct
      connection **before the body print commits the geometry**. Keeping the run
      short is the only defence the case can offer, and `pcb_x0` is where it is
      for that reason.
- [ ] **Can the µSD card be pulled out?** By construction it stops level with the
      floor of its finger pocket (0.5 mm proud), so what does the pulling is the
      3.5 mm of open slot either side of the card. That is the first thing to
      judge on the coupon. If a nail can't hook it, `sd_slot_w` has 8 mm of room
      to grow before it meets the keyboard port's pocket, and `sd_pocket_w` has
      the same.
- [ ] **The USB-C shells stand inside their openings.** The mouths sit 0.5 mm into
      the wall's thickness, which is what buys the plug its engagement — but it
      also means `port_fit` (0.5 mm a side) is the board's **alignment** budget in
      X and Z, not just the plug's clearance. Dry-fit the coupon on the
      real board before the body print; if a shell binds, `port_fit` grows and
      nothing else has to move.
- [ ] **The devkit stack is the one unmeasured number in the cavity.** `pcb_h` =
      13.5 is socket 8.5 + board 1.6 + can 3.4, none of it calipered. It clears
      the deck by 19 mm, so it is not load-bearing — but it is what the ceiling
      assert is checked against, and the board is in hand.
- [ ] **Re-check `pwr_fit = 0.4` on the coupon.** At 0.4 the hole is Ø13.9 and
      the bearing against `pwr_body_d` is **0.05 mm a side** — thin, and that
      bearing is what retains the switch. If it comes out loose, `pwr_fit` wants
      about **0.05**. The switch is
      deliberately far tighter than the ports: it is the one part *retained* by
      the panel rather than merely passing through it, and at `pwr_fit = 1.2`
      the hole printed Ø14.7, wider than `pwr_body_d`, and the panel stopped
      retaining it at all.
- [ ] **The 12 heat-sets are the one irreversible step on a print.** The geometry
      is checked (walls, skin, roof, floor and thread engagement are asserted; the
      screw and insert-body envelopes were booleaned against the solids and come
      back empty — the insert's head end has no relief, it melts its own seat by
      design), but nothing in the model can hold the **iron**. Two live risks:
      - **Over-pressing the bracket four.** The bore is 4.8 deep for a 3.8
        insert and has no stop in it, so there is 1 mm of slack under a flush
        insert and only **2.1 mm of deck** past that — on the face the user
        looks at. Sink to flush and stop; if a first deck dimples or blushes
        there, take `lip_t` up before touching anything else (each +1 is +1 of
        skin, and the only cost is a deeper bezel well).
      - **The screw length is a tape-measure datum.** Bore depths are budgeted
        at `scr_thread_max = 8` against a measured 7, so a 7.5 mm batch is
        already covered; a 10 mm one is not, and on the bracket it would arrive
        at the deck skin. Caliper one screw before driving 4 into the deck.
- [ ] **Programming means opening the case.** The board's own charge USB-C carries
      no data — the USB-serial bridge is on the devkit — so the port that flashes
      the machine is the devkit's, pointing into the cavity. Four screws take the
      baseplate off and the whole board assembly comes with it, which is the
      intended answer; OTA is the other one.
- [ ] Optional **hinged lid** over the deck (protects the glass in a bag) — not
      yet modelled.
- [ ] Feet — **deferred** (`feet_mode = "none"`). Modelled and ready as a
      separate part. With the heads flush there is nothing forcing them back:
      the plate sits flat on the desk on its own face.

## The panel measurement

Measured borders, glass edge → active area: **9.0 left** (FPC side) · **2.0
right** · **4.0 top** · **4.0 bottom**. From those,
`active_off = (left − right)/2` horizontally and `(top − bottom)/2` vertically,
giving **+3.5 mm horizontal** (toward the right, away from the FPC) and **0
vertical**.

Only the differences are used, so measuring to the visible light window rather
than the true pixel array is fine — the overshoot is symmetric and cancels. The
size itself comes from the datasheet, where it is exact: 792 × 272 px at 0.1755 mm
pitch = **139.00 × 47.74 mm**.

Consequences of the 2 mm right border: the bezel ledge over the glass is
**8.46 mm on the left and 1.46 mm on the right**. That ledge is a step in a solid
deck rather than a free-standing rib, so 1.46 mm prints and holds fine — the
bracket and foam behind carry the clamping load, and the ledge only has to stop
the glass falling forward.
